**Amodei (T2, T3)** says: The physics is real but the engineering is tractable. Constitutional principles, interpretability, red teaming, and measured autonomy expansion can keep it safe. We should build it carefully because someone will build it regardless.

**[Author] (FW)** says (through his work, not explicitly): Both are partially right, but neither has the evidence. I'm building the forensic infrastructure to measure what's actually happening — across platforms, across conversations, across behavioral patterns. The answer to whether control is possible isn't philosophical. It's empirical. And the data is in my database.

**The gap all three share:** None of them has solved the measurement problem. Aguirre has the math but no data. Amodei has the data but frames it through a commercial lens. [Author] has independent cross-platform behavioral data but hasn't yet built the analytical framework to answer the specific questions the other two are debating.

**[Author]'s unique position:** He is simultaneously a user of these systems (daily Claude interaction), a builder of tools on them (automation, patents), a forensic investigator of them (CHATS_MANAGER, HAR analysis, telemetry forensics), and a legal theorist about them (AICAF, FTC framework). No one else in this conversation holds all four positions at once.
